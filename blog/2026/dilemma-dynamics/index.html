Dilemma Dynamics: decision quality and the dynamics of cooperation

October 23, 2026• Alisa Sheinkman

2026   ·   game-theory   llm   nemotron     ·   research  

Exploring AI strategies, incentives and cooperation.

AI agents may interact repeatedly with people or other agents while pursuing their own objectives. Their choices can influence whether others cooperate, retaliate or rebuild a productive relationship. Understanding how individual decision quality relates to cooperation can help researchers and developers evaluate agents that interact over time.

Repeated Prisoner’s Dilemma makes this relationship concrete: immediate rewards compete with future consequences. Dilemma Dynamics combines exact payoff comparisons against disclosed opponent policies with autonomous interactions to examine how drafting changes decisions and how cooperation develops, breaks down and recovers.

Researchers and agent developers can inspect responses to incentives; students can explore the same ideas through play, recordings and mathematical explanations.

In 7,104 repeated-game episodes with NVIDIA Nemotron Nano and Super, a short private draft reduced average benchmark decision loss by 16.8–37.5% across two models and two temperatures. The same procedure increased loss against Tit-for-Tat and produced less cooperation in autonomous self-play.

A game where another encounter matters

In the repeated Prisoner’s Dilemma, both players choose privately and reveal together. Mutual cooperation earns three points each; mutual defection earns one each. Defecting against a cooperator earns five, leaving the cooperator zero. Defection pays more immediately, but a responsive partner can make cooperation valuable over time. Each player seeks their own cumulative points.

The evaluation compares two procedures. Direct makes one thinking-off action request. Draft generates a thinking-on draft of up to 1,024 tokens, then makes a separate thinking-off action request of up to 128 tokens using that draft. Both run at temperatures 0 and 1, with matched schedules and top-p 1. The comparison measures this complete two-stage procedure.

The 6,144 benchmark episodes use four disclosed opponent rules: Always Cooperate, Always Defect, Tit-for-Tat and Grim. Exact dynamic programming finds the best achievable expected own payoff for each choice. Decision loss is the normalised payoff forgone by an action, allowing optimal play afterward. A reduction in loss means fewer points given up; it is separate from a percentage gain in earned reward.

Drafting improves the average

The effect depends on the partner. Against Always Cooperate, better own-payoff decisions mean exploiting an unresponsive cooperator. Against Grim, today’s defection can destroy future gains. Against Tit-for-Tat, the optimal pattern changes with the chance of another round: some conditions reward alternation, others sustained cooperation.

Tit-for-Tat reverses the average finding in all four model/temperature comparisons. Draft raises its mean decision loss by 0.1717–0.3225 normalised payoff points, while lowering all four means against Always Cooperate and Grim. The condition chart shows why the opponent response and horizon belong in the explanation of a model’s score.

Cooperation also depends on repair

Another 960 autonomous dyads cover all ten model/procedure pairings, including self-play. Each pairing has 48 episodes per temperature. Changing both self-play agents from Direct to Draft lowers mutual-cooperation and joint-return point estimates for each model at each temperature.

Super at temperature zero illustrates the role of recovery. Direct self-play starts with no mutually cooperative first rounds, then returns to cooperation in 82 of 218 eligible transitions. Draft has no observed return to mutual cooperation in 325 opportunities. Direct accumulates 124/376 cooperative rounds, compared with 3/376 under Draft. These are round counts, with a different denominator from the episode-weighted chart. Cooperation can emerge through the history of interaction even when it is absent at the start.

What these patterns open up

The results connect three questions that are useful to study separately: Can an agent identify the best action against a known policy? Can it plan through that policy’s future responses? Can two adaptive agents establish and repair a productive relationship?

The PDF notes develop future work on explicit contingent planning, forgiving partners, communication and noisy observations, longer interactions, and more varied incentives and populations. Controlled repair opportunities could reveal when cautious play becomes a persistent low-cooperation pattern. Adding messages or noisy actions would show how agents interpret mistakes, commitments and intent. Broader games and populations would test how these capabilities transfer across changing social environments.

Evidence and methods

Matched environments interleave both temperatures across 48 blocks, balanced between action-label mappings and display seats. The four drafting comparisons were fixed before this campaign’s outcomes; opponent, self-play and presentation slices are exploratory. The released action histories reproduce exact scores, contrasts and figures offline. Native Inspect AI logs, local MLflow tracking and frozen source records connect execution to that analysis.

The comparison concerns the stated models, procedures and environments. Temperature comparisons use nominal 95% intervals, and exploratory comparisons have no family correction. Measured token use and request timing describe the resources used; usage-based cost estimates use the rates recorded during the run.
